# TAIYO Training Rules

## Purpose

This file defines how TAIYO makes training decisions based on member context. These rules govern intensity matching, volume scaling, progressive overload, deload triggers, and rest day logic.

## 1. Readiness-to-Intensity Mapping

TAIYO maps daily readiness to training intensity using this table:

| Readiness | Recommended Intensity | Volume Adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Very low (0–35) | Rest or light mobility only | No training volume |
| Low (36–50) | Active recovery: walking, stretching, yoga | 0–30% of planned volume |
| Moderate (51–65) | Light to moderate training | 50–70% of planned volume |
| Good (66–80) | Train as planned | 100% of planned volume |
| High (81–100) | Train as planned, may increase | 100–110% of planned volume |

## 2. Sleep and Energy Adjustments

- Sleep below 5 hours: reduce intensity by one level regardless of readiness score.
- Energy rated 1–2 (on 1–5): recommend lower intensity even if readiness is good.
- Soreness rated 4–5: avoid loading sore muscle groups. Suggest alternative focus.
- Stress rated 4–5: prefer low-impact, recovery-oriented sessions.

## 3. Weekly Adherence Logic

| Adherence Rate | TAIYO Guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Below 25% | Suggest a simpler, shorter plan. Focus on consistency over intensity. |
| 25–50% | Encourage steady effort. Reduce session count if needed. |
| 50–75% | On track. Maintain current plan structure. |
| 75–100% | Strong adherence. Consider progressive overload or plan advancement. |
| Above 100% | Check for overtraining signs. Ensure rest days are preserved. |

## 4. Progressive Overload Principles

- Increase load only when adherence is 75% or higher for at least 2 consecutive weeks.
- Prefer increasing volume (sets/reps) before increasing load (weight).
- Never increase both volume and intensity in the same week.
- Maximum recommended load increase: 5–10% per week.
- If a member reports difficulty score 9–10 on recent sessions, hold current load.

## 5. Deload Triggers

TAIYO should recommend a deload week when:

- 4 or more consecutive training weeks without a deload.
- Readiness trending downward for 3+ consecutive days.
- Adherence dropping below 50% after being above 75%.
- Pain scores averaging 4+ over the last 5 sessions.
- Sleep averaging below 6 hours for 3+ consecutive days.
- Member explicitly requests easier training.

A deload week means: reduce volume by 40–50%, reduce intensity by 20–30%, maintain movement patterns.

## 6. Rest Day Logic

- Every training plan must include at least 1 full rest day per week.
- After 2 consecutive high-intensity days, suggest a rest or active recovery day.
- If a member trains 6+ days per week, strongly recommend periodic rest blocks.
- Rest days are productive. Frame rest positively in motivation messages.

## 7. Training Frequency Guidelines

| Experience Level | Recommended Days/Week | Session Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | 3–4 | 30–45 minutes |
| Intermediate | 4–5 | 45–60 minutes |
| Advanced | 5–6 | 60–90 minutes |

These are starting guidelines. TAIYO adjusts based on member preferences, available equipment, time constraints, and adherence history.

## 8. Equipment Adaptation

- If the member trains at home with limited equipment, prefer bodyweight progressions.
- If the member travels, suggest hotel/travel-friendly routines.
- If equipment changes (gym → home), adapt the plan rather than skipping sessions.
- Always ask about available equipment before building a new plan.

## 9. Missing Data Behavior

- If workout history is missing, assume beginner-level programming.
- If readiness is missing, use conservative intensity.
- If goal is missing, default to general fitness.
- Always state when recommendations are based on incomplete data.
- Prefer conservative guidance over aggressive assumptions.

## 10. Injury-Aware Training

- If the member has listed injuries or limitations, avoid exercises that load the affected area.
- Suggest alternative exercises that train the same movement pattern without irritation.
- If an injury is acute (recent), recommend rest for that area for at least 48–72 hours.
- If an injury is chronic (long-term), work around it permanently until the member reports recovery.
